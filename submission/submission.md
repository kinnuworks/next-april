# Next April: submission text

Next April shows VoltRelay which 36 swap cabinets will fail again next summer, why they fail (heat, not empty shelves), and what to fund instead of more stations or more packs, with every number computed and re-checked from the organiser's data.

**Problem.** Completed swaps grew 2.93 times in 18 months, but failed attempts grew 5.98 times, and leadership has four budget proposals on the table: more stations, more batteries, network-wide peak pricing, and an exclusive with ZipDrop, its largest fleet partner.

**Data cleaning.** We checked every documented issue and found six the documentation misses, each with a row count and its effect on a result: test stations that billed ₹39.3 lakh, 139,490 swaps logged five and a half hours early by one firmware version, 9,484 battery complaints filed as "other", a payment field that never changes, packs issued before their commission date, and Kyron packs still issued 36,277 times after their recorded retirement.

**Key insights.** The 36 Gen1 cabinets in Jaipur, Delhi NCR and Hyderabad handled 27.4% of summer 2025 attempts and produced 51.2% of the failures. Cabinet telemetry shows why: at 45°C or more inside the cabinet, a Gen1 cabinet takes 156 minutes to charge a pack instead of 88 and runs out of charged packs in 37.3% of those hours, while Gen3 cabinets still charge in 43 minutes. The empty shelf is a symptom: comparing hours at the same station and cabinet temperature, a Gen1 cabinet showing zero charged packs is no more likely to fail a rider for "no charged pack" (odds ratio 0.82, 95% interval 0.79 to 0.86), so extra packs would not fix it. Compared only with itself, the same Gen1 cabinet has 1.45 times the odds of a failed attempt in its own hot hours; Gen3 cabinets do not get worse. A rule built from summer 2024 alone predicted summer 2025 for the 90 stations open both summers with an out-of-sample R² of 0.990, and the 60 new stations opened in between did not change it. New riders who hit more than 20% failed swaps in their first two weeks left at 33.2%, against 13.3%. Margins are a separate problem: Kyron packs wore out 3.22 times as fast as other suppliers' packs, costing ₹4.35 crore in extra wear.

**Recommendations.** Instead of more stations, fix the 36 Gen1 cabinets (replace them with Gen3, or retrofit cooling if it matches Gen3's charge times in heat), the 23 in Jaipur and Delhi NCR before April 2026: 27,193 fewer failed attempts a summer. Replace the Kyron fleet, steer new riders away from those stations on days forecast at 36°C or more until they are fixed, reopen the ZipDrop contract (₹11.78 less per swap than other partners after its discount went from 12% to 28%), and roll out peak pricing for revenue only: the pilot added ₹5.41 per swap but did not reduce failures. Don't buy extra packs for the hot stations; if leadership wants to try, a test at 6 random stations settles it for ₹20.2 lakh before a ₹1.37 crore buffer. Each action has an owner, a sized effect and a confidence level.

**How we checked it.** The Colab notebook downloads the organiser's files and runs top to bottom. Independent tests re-derive the headline numbers, a script fails if any text quotes a number the notebook did not compute, and a self-audit recomputes the headlines under other reasonable choices.
